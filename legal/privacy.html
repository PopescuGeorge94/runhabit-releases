1.4. This policy is written in line with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the “GDPR”), Romanian Law No. 190/2018, Romanian Law No. 506/2004 and other applicable rules. It provides the information required by Articles 13 and 14 GDPR.

2. Definitions

In short: The GDPR terms, in plain words.

Personal data
any information about an identified or identifiable person, such as a name, a Garmin account ID or an IP address.
Processing
anything done with data: collecting, storing, reading, using, sending, deleting.
Controller
whoever decides why and how data is processed.
Processor
whoever processes data on the controller’s behalf.
Data concerning health
data about physical or mental health, such as heart rate, HRV, sleep, stress, respiration, weight, menstrual cycle, pain or symptoms (Article 4(15) GDPR). It is a special category of data (Article 9).
Explicit consent
your agreement given through a separate, clear and express statement, such as ticking the box next to the text that says exactly what you are agreeing to.
Consent (acord)
what you give on the consent screen: acceptance of the Terms of Use, explicit consent for data concerning health and, if you also tick the optional form entry box, consent to reading and sending the form entry. “Retrage acordul” (Withdraw consent, under Ceasul › Despre) withdraws all of it for your profile (Article 7(3) GDPR) and ends the contract. The form entry alone is switched on or off separately, under Ceasul › Despre › “Trimite rândul către dezvoltator” (send the entry to the developer).
Profiling
the automated evaluation of personal aspects, such as your state of health or fitness (Article 4(4) GDPR).
EEA
the European Economic Area: the EU Member States plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway.
Installation code
a random 16-character code generated on your phone the first time the app runs. It is not derived from your name or accounts, but, if you choose to send the form entry, it is sent to the developer together with your Garmin account ID and name (section 4.1); it stays the same while the app is installed.
Control file
the public file on GitHub that tells the app whether it has been switched off and which announcements or new versions exist. For switch-offs and exceptions it contains hashes of installation codes and Garmin account IDs, never names.
Hash
a fingerprint calculated from an ID and a fixed value built into the app (a salt). It is pseudonymised data, and so still personal data: the salt is inside the app and Garmin IDs are numbers, so someone who extracts the salt from the app can check whether a given account is on the list.
Profile
a person’s separate space in the app; a phone can have up to four.

3. How the app is built

In short: Your data is analysed on your phone. The developer receives only five fields, and only if you choose to send them; other providers receive only what they need.

3.1. RunHabit has no server. Data fetched from Garmin and data you enter is stored and analysed in the app’s private storage on your phone.

3.2. The developer cannot see your runs, routes, health data or profile form.

3.3. The only things that leave your phone are:

  • (a) the form entry sent to the developer, only if you have chosen it and while sending is switched on (section 4.1);
  • (b) requests to Garmin, Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap, Open Food Facts and GitHub, described in section 7;
  • (c) whatever you choose to send: a backup, your journal, images, codes, plans, emails;
  • (d) your phone’s automatic Google backup, if you have it switched on and your phone has a screen lock;
  • (e) if you installed the app from Google Play: what Google Play itself collects under Google’s policies, including, if you allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, crash and performance reports, which Google shows the developer in aggregated form in the Play Console.

3.4. The app contains no ads, no usage analytics, no cross-app tracking and no advertising networks. We do not sell, rent or share your data for marketing.

3.5. The app does not ask for access to your phone’s location, contacts, camera, microphone or photo gallery: you choose a photo yourself through the Android picker. For weather, it uses the city you type in your profile form.

3.6. On Google Play (“Data safety”), “collected” means any data that leaves your phone, even if the developer never receives it. That is why it lists: your name, Garmin account ID and installation code, with the app version and phone model (the form entry, optional); the error log, only when you send it; your city (to Open-Meteo) and the area of your routes (map tiles, to OpenStreetMap); training and health data written to your Garmin account, only when you tap. Everything is sent encrypted (HTTPS).

4. What data we process and where it comes from

In short: Most data comes from your Garmin account and stays on your phone. Only five fields reach the developer, and only if you choose to send them.

4.1. The entry sent to the developer

The installation code, the app version, your numeric Garmin account ID, the name on your Garmin account (your full name or, if there is none, your display name) and your phone’s make and model. Without a connected account, the ID and name are left blank. The entry is optional: it is sent only if you tick its box on the consent screen or later switch sending on under Ceasul › Despre › “Trimite rândul către dezvoltator”, and only while sending is on. It is then sent when you accept (if switched on later, the next time you open the app), when you install a new version and when you connect a new account, one per account, not every time you open the app. On a phone with several profiles, a person’s account and name are sent only with that person’s choice; the entry without an account (the installation code, the version and the phone, which are the same for everyone) is sent if at least one person who has accepted has chosen to send it. It contains nothing about your health or your runs, and never your Garmin password or token.

4.2. Data from your Garmin account, fetched to your phone

  • your account: ID, name, watches and sensors, settings (such as heart rate zones);
  • activities: type, date, duration, distance, GPS route, pace, heart rate, power, cadence, running dynamics, elevation, temperature, laps, training effect, training load, calories, the weather recorded by Garmin, shoes, photos, records, FIT files;
  • daily and health data: resting and all-day heart rate, HRV, sleep and sleep stages, Body Battery, stress, respiration, blood oxygen (if your watch measures it), steps, floors, intensity minutes, weight and body composition, VO2 max, lactate threshold, predictions and training status;
  • what you log in Garmin: calories eaten (including from MyFitnessPal), water intake, menstrual cycle (only if you turn the feature on), calendar, races, courses.

4.3. Data you enter

Your profile form (profile name, city, sex, age, wake-up and running times, race and target, results, foods you avoid), your plan and changes to it, effort and pain logged after runs, your morning answers to “Cum te simți?” (“How do you feel?”: fatigue, legs, wish to run), your answers to “Sunt răcit azi” (“I have a cold”), including fever and antibiotics, meals, strength and CrossFit sessions (movements, weights, result, duration, effort, notes), breaks, the track lane you chose, photos you add, and your settings.

4.4. Other people’s data

Your Garmin connections and their runs (in Prieteni (Friends), displayed only), the data of people you follow with a code they gave you (Urmărești (Following)), names in challenge invitations, and codes you receive. Details in section 13.

4.5. Technical data

On your phone: the error and event log (without tokens), network status, settings, the installation code and the proof of your consent (section 5.2). The third-party services the app contacts see your IP address, the time and the usual technical details of an internet request.

4.6. Emails and donations

If you write to us, we receive your address and what you write, including the text from “Trimite problema” (report a problem) or the error log, if you choose to send it. If you donate, Revolut shows the developer the data provided for by Revolut’s rules, usually your name and the amount.

5. Purposes and legal bases

In short: We process data to provide the app’s features, to keep the app secure and to answer you. Health data is processed on the basis of your explicit consent, and the form entry is optional: it is sent only if you consent and is kept on the basis of our legitimate interests.

5.1. Performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). The features you request under the Terms of Use: signing in, fetching and analysing data, the plan, writing to Garmin, weather, maps, calories and updates.

5.2. Explicit consent for data concerning health (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR and Article 3(1) of Romanian Law No. 190/2018). You give it on the consent screen by ticking the health data box. It covers the data fetched from Garmin, the data you log yourself, the calculations made from it (section 5.6), the automatic Google backup and anything written to your Garmin account. For the menstrual cycle feature, you also give your explicit consent when you turn it on in your profile form; you can turn it off at any time in the same place. You can withdraw your consent at any time under Ceasul › Despre › Retrage acordul; after that, the app stops for your profile. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before it (Article 7(3)). As proof of your consent (Article 7(1)), we keep on your phone the date and time you accepted, the consent version, the age option you chose and whether you ticked the form entry box; you can see them under Ceasul › Despre and send them to us if needed.

5.3. The form entry. The form entry is optional. The app reads the five fields from your phone and sends them only with your consent (Article 4(5)(a) of Romanian Law No. 506/2004): you tick their optional box on the consent screen or later switch sending on under Ceasul › Despre › “Trimite rândul către dezvoltator”. It is not a condition for using the app (Article 7(4) GDPR): without it, the app works just the same, and only the entry does not leave your phone. You can withdraw your consent at any time by switching sending off in the same place, and the app keeps working; withdrawal does not affect entries sent before. With every new consent, for example after “Retrage acordul” or for a new version of the texts, you choose again, with the same box. We keep the entries we receive on the basis of our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Our interests are: (a) managing access to the app, meaning switching it on or off for a specific person, installation or account, for example after abuse, at Garmin’s request or when the app is open only to some people; for this we need to know who an account or installation belongs to, which is why we also receive the name on your Garmin account; (b) version compatibility and fixing bugs (the version and the phone’s make and model); (c) recognising you when you write to us about your rights, and being able to defend our rights. We have weighed these interests against yours: the data is minimal, contains nothing about health or runs, only the developer sees it, we keep only the latest entry (section 9.2), and we put in the public file only the hashes a rule needs, removing them once the rule is no longer needed. You can object at any time (Article 21), as described in section 10.

We also read the control file on the basis of our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR): keeping the app secure and switching it off for everyone or for one installation or account. No data leaves your phone when it is read; GitHub only sees your IP address, as any website does.

5.4. Consent of the people you follow (Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a) GDPR). The run summaries of a person you follow include health data (heart rate, heart rate zones, VO2 max, calories). They are kept on your phone on the basis of that person’s explicit consent, given in their own app when they tap “Lasă pe cineva să te urmărească” (Let someone follow you), after the app has told them what the code shares. They can withdraw it by asking you to tap “Nu mai urmări” (Stop following), which deletes their data from your phone, or by removing you from their Garmin connections or hiding their activities, after which your app receives nothing new about them. When they send you their plan, they can tick boxes to send health data and other personal data with it (section 7.8); that data is kept on your phone on the basis of their explicit consent, given through those boxes, until you tap “Nu mai urmări” (Stop following).

5.5. Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR). Responding to your requests about your rights and to requests from authorities, keeping proof of consent, and any tax obligations relating to donations.

5.6. Profiling. The app automatically evaluates, on your phone, your health and training data (such as HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, training load and runs) to calculate your morning score, fatigue signs, predictions and plan. This is profiling (Article 4(4) GDPR) and happens only with your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR and Article 3(1) of Romanian Law No. 190/2018). In short, your values today are compared with your own values over recent weeks, and the plan follows training rules built into the app. We do not make decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affect you (Article 22). The results are for your information, and the decision is yours. The remote switch-off is decided by the developer, not by an algorithm.

5.7. You are not obliged to give us any data. Without accepting the Terms and without consent for health data, however, the app cannot work, because analysing your data is what it does. The form entry is optional: without it, the app works just the same, and you can switch the sending on or off at any time (section 5.3).

7. Who receives data

In short: Each service gets only what it needs for its job. We never sell data.

7.1. Google (Google Forms and Google Sheets). The form entry lands in the developer’s personal Google account, where only he can see it. The service is provided in the EEA by Google Ireland Limited under Google’s Terms and Privacy Policy. For personal accounts, Google does not sign a processor agreement and processes the data under its own terms, including for security; for the technical details of the request (IP address, time, browser), Google acts on its own behalf, as a controller. The form is submitted without a Google sign-in. The same applies to emails sent to the developer’s Gmail address.

7.2. Garmin. Once you are signed in, the app sends your requests to Garmin using your token: reads and, only when you tap, writes. Garmin is an independent controller for the data in your account, under its own policy.

7.3. GitHub (GitHub, Inc., part of Microsoft). The control file is read each time the app opens, every 5 minutes while it is on screen, and at most once an hour in the background. The request does not say who you are; GitHub sees your IP address, as any website does. Only in versions installed from outside Google Play is a new version downloaded from the app’s public Releases, when you tap “Actualizează” (update). The Google Play version is updated only through Google Play.

7.4. Open-Meteo (OpenMeteo GmbH, Switzerland). The city name from your profile form, to find its coordinates, and then the coordinates, for the forecast. According to the provider, its server logs are deleted after 90 days.

7.5. OpenStreetMap (OpenStreetMap Foundation, United Kingdom). Map tiles for the area of your route, served through a global delivery network. The tiles requested reveal the map area.

7.6. Open Food Facts (an association based in France). Only the name of the food you search for, when you tap search.

7.7. Google (automatic Android backup). If you have it switched on in your phone settings, Android stores in your Google account your profile form, settings, plan, the Garmin account ID linked to your profile and summaries of your runs, days and weeks, without your Garmin token, photos, FIT files or run details. The backup also contains health data (sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, weight, blood oxygen, heart rate during runs) and is sent only when it can be end-to-end encrypted with your phone’s screen lock (PIN, pattern or password). On a phone without a screen lock, it is not sent. The developer cannot see this backup.

7.8. Apps you share through. When you send a backup, your journal, an image, a code, a plan or an email, the data goes to the app and the recipient you choose. When you send your plan to a friend, you can choose to tick boxes that also send health data and other personal data with it: the meals in the descriptions (your weight can be worked out from their grams), your bedtime and wake-up time, what you do not eat, your sick breaks, the pains you noted, your HRV, sleep and resting heart rate for the last 4 weeks, the effort you noted and your run notes. Your notes are sent exactly as you wrote them. Without a tick none of it is sent, and the phase of your cycle is never sent. What you tick is sent once, as a copy from the day you send it, in the plan file (.runhabit). The file is text that can be read without RunHabit, and it stays wherever you send it: in the chat or email, in the app you sent it through and on the phones of the people who receive it, until each of them deletes it there. “Nu mai urmări” (Stop following), tapped by your friend, deletes only what their RunHabit app kept.

7.9. Revolut. Donations are made on Revolut’s page, in your browser, under Revolut’s rules.

7.10. Authorities. Only where the law requires us to.

7.11. The developer has no processor agreements. For the data he is responsible for, he uses these service providers: Google (Forms, Sheets and Gmail, for the form entries and emails) and GitHub (for the control file). They process the data under their own terms (sections 7.1 and 7.3). There are no recipients other than those in sections 7.1 to 7.10.

8. Transfers outside the European Economic Area

In short: Google and GitHub may process data in the United States, with the safeguards the GDPR requires.

8.1. Google Ireland Limited, together with Google LLC, and GitHub, Inc. (Microsoft) may store and process data in the United States. These transfers rely on the European Commission’s adequacy decision for the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795 of 10 July 2023), for certified companies, and on the standard contractual clauses approved by the Commission (Article 46 GDPR), as described by those providers. The list of certified companies is at https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/list, and the standard contractual clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) are at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/914/oj. Google and GitHub state that they also use these clauses, which would continue to apply if the adequacy decision were annulled. On request, we will tell you which safeguards each provider describes.

8.2. Switzerland and the United Kingdom, where Open-Meteo and the OpenStreetMap Foundation are based, are covered by European Commission adequacy decisions. Open Food Facts is based in France. The delivery networks these providers use may have servers in other countries too.

8.3. Garmin describes its own transfers in its privacy policy.

9. How long data is kept

In short: On your phone, for as long as you like. With the developer, if you have chosen to send it, only the latest form entry, for up to 24 months (longer only in case of abuse), and emails for up to 3 years after the last message.

9.1. Data on your phone stays there until you delete it: through the app, by clearing its data in Android settings, or by uninstalling it. The developer cannot delete it remotely, because he has no access to it. The proof of consent is deleted when you withdraw your consent.

9.2. Without your choice, we receive no entry with your account or name, and if nobody on the phone has chosen to send it, no entry from that phone. For each installation and each account we keep only the latest entry. Older entries are deleted in a clean-up we carry out at least every 6 months. The latest entry is kept for up to 24 months after we receive it and is then deleted. If an installation or account has been switched off because of abuse, we keep the entry and the rule for as long as the switch-off remains in force and, after it is lifted, for up to 3 years for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims (Article 2517 of the Romanian Civil Code). We review once a year whether the switch-off is still needed.

9.3. Hashes in the control file stay there as long as the rule is needed, and are then removed.

9.4. Emails are kept as long as needed to answer you and, after the last message, for up to 3 years (the general limitation period, Article 2517 of the Romanian Civil Code) for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims. Our answers to requests about your rights are kept for 3 years after the answer, as proof.

9.5. Donation records (the name and amount, as shown by Revolut) stay in the transaction history of the developer’s Revolut account for as long as Revolut keeps them under its rules and the law. The developer does not copy them anywhere else and uses them only to keep track of donations and for any tax obligations, for the 5-year tax limitation period (Article 110 of Romanian Law No. 207/2015 on the Fiscal Procedure Code).

9.6. Data held by third-party services (Garmin, Google, GitHub, Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap, Open Food Facts, Revolut) is kept under their own policies.

10. Your rights

In short: You can ask to see, correct, delete or take your data, and you can object. It is free, and we normally answer within one month.

10.1. Under Articles 15 to 22 GDPR, you have the right:

  • of access: to know whether we process data about you and to receive a copy;
  • to rectification: to have inaccurate data corrected;
  • to erasure (the “right to be forgotten”);
  • to restriction of processing;
  • to data portability: to receive your data in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and to transmit it to another controller; for the data on your phone, you already have the backup and the FIT and GPX export;
  • to object (Article 21), in particular to processing based on legitimate interests, on grounds relating to your particular situation: you can stop new entries under Ceasul › Despre › “Trimite rândul către dezvoltator”, or write to us, and the app keeps working. We delete the entries you have already sent and, if you write to us, also any further entries that arrive from your installation or account, unless, in your specific situation, we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests (for example ongoing abuse) or need them to establish, exercise or defend legal claims; if so, we tell you what they are;
  • to withdraw consent at any time: for the form entry, under Ceasul › Despre › “Trimite rândul către dezvoltator”, and the app works just the same; for the whole consent, under Ceasul › Despre › Retrage acordul; withdrawal does not affect earlier processing;
  • not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing (section 5.6).

10.2. Write to us at georgepopescu594@gmail.com. So that we do not hand your data to someone pretending to be you, we may ask for your installation code (shown under Ceasul › Despre, in “Ce primește dezvoltatorul”) or your Garmin account ID.

10.3. We answer without undue delay and within one month of receiving your request. For complex or numerous requests, this can be extended by two further months, and we will tell you why (Article 12(3)). Answering is free, unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

10.4. The data on your phone is under your control: you can view it in the app, export it and delete it yourself. The developer has no access to it.

10.5. 11. How to have your data deleted

15. Security

In short: The app does not read or keep your Garmin password, the token is encrypted, the Google backup is sent only encrypted, and updates are checked before installation.

15.1. Our measures:

  • you sign in on Garmin’s official page; the app does not read, store or transmit your password, and only takes the final sign-in ticket from the page;
  • the Garmin token is encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore, which cannot be exported from your phone;
  • all connections to services use HTTPS;
